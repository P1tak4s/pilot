# Effort for helpers: on vs off (2026-10-10)

Opus 5.5, app effort high, effortless loaded with Auto on in both setups. Only the Helpers setting differs:
off = subagents run at the app's effort (high), on = the judge picks each subagent's effort before it starts.
4 tasks that make Claude send off subagents, 2 runs each per setup, 16 runs, billed to an API key.
Run it again with `bash bench/helpers/run.sh opus high 2 4` then `node bench/helpers/score.cjs`.

## What it shows

- Helpers on cost 10% less per task overall: 26-28% less on the two small lookup tasks, 5% less on the rest.
- The big task (s3, tracing the update flow) is most of the cost. There the judge gave the helpers high, the same as off, so little changed.
- Two of eight runs with Helpers on were wrong: both counted test calls with a low-effort helper that also counted a regex .test( call (186, truth 185). With Helpers off, both runs had it right. Low effort on a helper can cost accuracy on counting jobs.
- 16 runs is small. The 10% is a direction, not a figure to publish.

## Rerun: helpers start at medium (dev 1.69.3)

Same tasks, Opus at high, Helpers on only, 8 runs (the off runs above are the baseline). Raw rows: helpers-medium-2026-10-10.json.

| Task | Off avg | On avg (medium floor) | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| s1 | $0.272 | $0.357 | +31% |
| s2 | $0.224 | $0.260 | +16% |
| s3 | $1.599 | $1.265 | -21% |
| s4 | $0.173 | $0.237 | +37% |
| all | $0.567 | $0.529 | -7% |

- 8 of 8 correct (the count is right now). The judge gave helpers medium 10 times, high 2 times.
- The small tasks came out dearer than off even though their helpers ran at medium instead of high. With 2 runs per task the noise is bigger than the effect: one big task (s3) decides the total.
- No evidence yet that Helpers saves money. It stays off by default and out of the marketing. A real answer needs more runs (about 5 per task, roughly $25 on the key).
