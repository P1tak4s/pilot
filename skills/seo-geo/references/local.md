# Local SEO

For businesses that serve people in a place: shops, clinics, services, trades, restaurants.

## Google Business Profile (GBP)

- The **primary category** is the strongest single local factor; a wrong one is the most common
  self-inflicted loss. Pick the most specific category that describes the core service; add a few
  accurate secondary ones.
- Complete every field: name exactly as used in the real world (no keyword stuffing; it is a
  suspension risk), address or service area, hours (including holiday hours), phone, website with UTM
  tags, services or products with descriptions, attributes, opening date, photos (real, recent,
  geotag irrelevant), posts.
- Verify the profile and keep owner access in the business's own account.
- Q&A on profiles has been removed; move common questions to the website's FAQ.

## Reviews

- Count, rating, recency and replies all matter. Aim for a steady flow (a gap of weeks hurts more than
  a lower average), 10+ reviews as a first milestone, 4.5+ average, and a reply to every review within
  a few days, negative ones first.
- Ask every customer the same way (link or QR after the service). **Review gating** (asking only happy
  customers, or filtering first) breaks Google's policy and, in several countries, consumer law.
- Never buy, swap or write reviews. Suspicious patterns (bursts of 5-star reviews, one-review
  accounts, identical wording) get filtered and can trigger penalties.

## NAP and citations

- Name, address and phone are identical everywhere: website footer, contact page, schema, GBP,
  Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing Places and local directories. Small differences (St. vs Street, old phone
  numbers) dilute trust.
- List the business in the main maps (Google, Apple, Bing) and the strongest local and industry
  directories; quality beats quantity.
- LocalBusiness schema on the site matches the GBP exactly (see `schema.md`).

## Location and service-area pages

- One strong page per real location, with content that only fits that place: address and map, local
  team, directions and parking, local photos, reviews from that area, local services and prices.
- Swap-the-city test: if replacing the city name is the only difference between two pages, they are
  doorway pages. Aim for 60%+ unique content per page.
- 30+ location pages needs a review of their quality; 50+ needs a written reason. URL pattern
  `/locations/city/` or `/service/city/`.
- Service-area businesses without a storefront hide the address in GBP and do not create a page per
  suburb they do not actually serve.

## Measuring local

- GBP insights (calls, direction requests, website clicks), GSC queries with city names, and a geo-grid
  rank check (for example a 7x7 grid over ~5km) for the main 3 to 5 queries, repeated monthly.
