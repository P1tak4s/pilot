# Content, intent and on-page

## Match the intent first

- Search the query and look at what ranks. If more than ~60% of the top results are one page type
  (guide, product, category, comparison, tool, local pack), that is the intent; 40 to 60% is mixed,
  under 40% is fragmented and leaves room to choose.
- A page type that does not match the SERP rarely ranks, however good it is: a blog post for a
  product query or a product page for an informational one is the first thing to fix.
- The answer to the query is visible in the first screen. A reader should know within ~10 seconds
  whether this page solves their problem.

## E-E-A-T without theatre

E-E-A-T is how Google's raters judge quality, not a ranking switch. Show it with facts:

- **Who**: a visible author or company with a real bio, credentials where they matter, and a
  profile that can be checked (LinkedIn, a company register).
- **How**: how the content was made (tested, measured, interviewed, used for N years), with first-hand
  photos, data or screenshots instead of stock.
- **Why**: written to help the reader decide or do something, not to fill a keyword slot.
- Trust: contact details, address, policies, sources linked, dates of last real update.
- Money and health topics (YMYL) need named experts and sources; unedited AI text is a liability there.

## On-page basics

- **Title**: about 50 to 60 characters, the topic and intent first, brand at the end. Unique per page.
- **Meta description**: about 120 to 160 characters, a concrete promise and a call to action. It earns
  clicks; it does not rank. Google rewrites weak ones.
- **H1**: one per page, matches the title's intent; H2/H3 follow the reader's questions, no skipped
  levels.
- The main topic appears naturally in the title, H1, URL, first 100 words and one subheading. Keep the
  exact keyword under ~2% of words; above ~3% reads as stuffing.
- **URL**: short, lowercase, hyphens, readable, no dates, stable.
- **Readability**: sentences around 15 to 20 words on average, paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences, plain
  words. Readability scores are a proxy, not a ranking factor.
- **Freshness**: review fast-moving topics at least yearly and show the real last-updated date.

## Length is coverage, not a target

Rough floors before a page can cover its topic: home ~500 words, service ~800, product ~300 to 400
(more for complex products), category ~400 of useful text, blog guide ~1,500, pillar 2,500 to 4,000.
Shorter is fine when it fully answers the query; longer is waste when it repeats.

## Briefs

A brief names: the query and intent, the page type the SERP rewards, the reader and their job, the
questions to answer, the **information gain** (what this page adds that the top results lack: data,
experience, a tool, a clearer method), sources to cite, internal links in and out, and the title and
meta. Client-facing briefs do not name the tools or frameworks used to make them.

## Clusters and internal links

- Group keywords by **SERP overlap**, not by wording: share 7 to 10 of the top-10 URLs means one page;
  4 to 6 means the same cluster; 2 to 3 means separate pages that link to each other; 0 to 1 means
  unrelated. When unsure, keep them together.
- Hub and spoke: a pillar page per topic, spokes for subtopics; every spoke links to the pillar and the
  pillar links to every spoke. Each page gets at least 3 internal links in.
- Roughly 3 to 10 internal links per 1,000 words, with descriptive anchors (not "click here").
- Two pages targeting the same intent cannibalise each other: merge them and 301 the weaker one.
- Breadcrumbs mirror the URL path.

## Comparison and alternative pages

- "X vs Y" and "X alternatives" pages are legitimate when fair: verifiable feature claims, prices with
  "as of [date]", own affiliation disclosed, and a real recommendation by use case.
- Review them at least quarterly; stale prices destroy trust.

## E-commerce pages

- Product: unique description (not the manufacturer's), title under ~60 characters, at least 3 images,
  price, availability, shipping and returns visible, real reviews.
- Category pages are often the strongest commercial pages: a short intro, filters that do not create
  indexable duplicates, and links to subcategories.
- Out-of-stock: keep the page with alternatives if it returns; 301 or 410 if it is gone for good.
